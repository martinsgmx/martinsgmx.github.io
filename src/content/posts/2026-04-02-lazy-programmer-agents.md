# Being a lazy programmer

Sometimes, when I'm coding something useful, I already think: well, now, in the future when I
need to improve this, maybe I need to refresh my memory on how this works, and it's not about touching documentation,
it's about how to put the development environment ready to code, and don't tell me about writing code, code, code, it's
about keeping my mind thinking about how to solve a specific problem or improve something. It's about being a lazy programmer :)

> I really appreciate when I don't forget anything that, with time, becomes obfuscated and tricky. My pragmatic side doesn't forget me!

## AI Agents Era

Now, when I interact with `copilot`, `kimi`, or `opencode`, I try to keep my requirements and plans under
a lazy programmer mindset: explain once, iterate twice. So, I try to explain everything and just create checkpoints
that must be reused in some lifecycle session. This way, I think just like any agent can read any script and
understand the main idea, something like: `BYPASS_MODE=1 make up` that calls something else, and so on.

## When the context matters

Now, context is king in the inference world. So, how can being a lazy programmer be useful?
When you're thinking you just need to recap any project or environment, just put the essential and avoid anything else, just the
pragmatic way. Replace the long explanation with a simple review of any script or any structure.
Now, it's a dirty way because that's the main reason that software engineering tries to solve, document, and
create laws or rules to avoid mistakes and overengineering. It's not about rewriting the way to write code, it's about
how to keep the main ideas aligned to new trends and tools without putting everything under one sheet.
